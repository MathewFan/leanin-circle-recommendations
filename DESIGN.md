# Circle recommendations — technical design

**Goal.** Help members find relevant, active communities they can actually join, including members with no profile or history. This implementation provides a local HTTP API backed by SQLite and the supplied synthetic snapshot. Python's standard library keeps setup to two commands, with no external services or credentials.

**Flow.** `GET /users/{id}/recommendations` validates the request, reads the member and candidate Circles, filters eligibility in SQL, scores candidates, and selects a diverse list. Each response includes the snapshot time and algorithm version. A connection per request avoids cross-thread SQLite use. Relational constraints protect references; membership and discovery indexes support the main lookups. Only eligible candidates are scored in memory. Membership-topic inference is skipped when stated interests exist.

**Eligibility and relevance.** Exclude existing memberships, unlisted or full Circles, activity older than 60 days, future activity, and zero 30-day activity. Stated interests drive primary-topic and weaker tag matches. Without them, membership topics provide a weaker fallback; without either, bounded activity and open joining provide exploration. Profile completeness, photos, and inferred demographic identity receive no weight. Geography is not scored because Circle meeting locations are missing; format is returned without claiming local fit.

**Ranking and explanations.** Primary-topic relevance earns 65 points, tag overlap 39, and an additional matched interest 5. Membership-derived relevance gets 35% of those points. Activity adds up to 25 using capped logarithmic post/chat volume and decaying recency; open joining adds 5. These deliberately simple weights need validation. Greedy selection penalizes repeated primary topics by 15 and caps each topic at two results. Ties use Circle ID. Explanations state an actual matching topic or exploration rationale, observed activity, and the join policy. They never claim promotion outcomes or compatibility not supported by the data.

**Tradeoffs.** SQLite and exact taxonomy matching favor auditability over semantic recall. Topic diversity handles the planted duplicates but cannot recognize cross-topic duplicates and may suppress distinct communities with a shared topic. Tags may be noisy. Aggregate activity can favor large or noisy groups despite caps; it measures participation, not safety or support quality. The supplied member count governs capacity without recounting memberships per request. Raw posts and profile fields are preserved but not used in ranking. The fixed September 21 snapshot anchors recency so the demo stays reproducible; production must refresh timestamps and aggregates together.

**Validation.** Twelve automated tests exercise HTTP success/errors, all 100 members, and the four planted scenarios. Additional boundaries cover uncapped/full Circles, public-but-inactive Circles, chat-only activity, future timestamps, empty pools, fallback interests, oversized IDs, exact activity-window boundaries, and deterministic results. These are correctness checks, not evidence of improved member outcomes.

**Production next.** Authenticate members and authorize visibility; recheck capacity transactionally when joining. Move to Postgres, retrieve a bounded candidate set through indexed topic/tag joins, batch fresh activity aggregates, and cache briefly by member and data version. Add timeouts, rate limits, monitoring, and exposure logging. Collect meeting locations and explicit preferences. Evaluate relevance and duplicate rate with member review, then test joins and sustained participation with guardrails for cold-start members and smaller Circles. Semantic similarity and feedback-based ranking follow only if they improve those outcomes without inferring sensitive identity.
